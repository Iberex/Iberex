## The concept

*Antwerp is Calling* turns the city into a conversation. Giant phones are placed on benches all over Antwerp. Scan the QR code, pick up, and you hear a raw, unscripted voice note from the last person who sat there. No tourist traps, no corporate scripts.

Every voice note ends with a question. Answer it, leave a new one behind, and your voice becomes part of an endless chain of strangers across the city.

## The experience

- **The call:** your phone rings: *"getting a call from Antwerp"*. Slide to answer and see how many people picked up this phone before you.
- **Listen & answer:** hear their story (AI-translated into your language if needed), then record your own answer to the question they left.
- **Join the chain:** leave a question for the next person and become part of the city.
- **Beyond the bench:** a proximity meter guides you to the nearest phone, *live messages* show what people are asking right now, and a podcast collects the best stories.

## My role

In our team, someone else designed the look and feel. I built it. My job was to take the design and turn it into something that actually works, on a real phone, on a real bench in the city.

The part I cared about most was the link between the physical and the digital. Scanning the QR code on a bench has to open the right phone, play the voice note that was left at that exact spot, and store every new answer and question so the next visitor hears it. I made that whole chain work, from the scan to the recording to everything that gets saved.

I also translated our visual style into code. The bold, playful identity from Figma had to feel exactly the same on a phone in the street, so every color, animation and interaction was built to match the design.

## Result

A working product, not just a prototype: the full mobile experience on location and a website to explore the stories from anywhere, made for Devine and the city of Antwerp.

It was also one of my first group projects where the teamwork just clicked. Everyone knew their part, we helped each other whenever someone got stuck, and we could build on each other's ideas without having to explain everything twice.
